MY GROUP WORK REFLECTION

Collaboration is a skill
we grow into.

Group work involves more than dividing tasks. It requires listening, cooperation, accountability and a willingness to learn from others.

Working in a group can bring together different personalities, experiences, strengths and ideas. These differences can make collaboration valuable, but they can also require patience, communication and compromise.

As a student, I recognise that group assignments provide opportunities to develop skills that are useful both academically and professionally. Teamwork can help students share knowledge, consider alternative perspectives and work towards a common objective.

Tuckman's model of group development provides a useful way to understand how teams may develop over time. Its five stages are Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing and Adjourning. Reflecting on these stages helps me understand what effective teamwork involves and what I can continue improving as a team member.

LOOKING CLOSER

Understanding each stage
of the journey.

01

THE BEGINNING

Forming: Establishing the Foundation

The forming stage is when team members come together and begin understanding the purpose of their task. At this stage, members may still be getting to know one another and learning about each person's strengths, expectations and responsibilities.

I believe that a group should begin by making sure everyone understands the task requirements, deadlines and expected outcome. Agreeing on responsibilities early can reduce confusion later. Introducing ideas respectfully and giving every member an opportunity to contribute can also help establish a positive working environment.

♡

My lesson: A clear beginning creates a stronger foundation for teamwork.

02

LEARNING TO DISAGREE

Storming: Managing Differences

During the storming stage, members may have different opinions about how a task should be completed. Differences in communication styles, expectations or approaches can create misunderstandings. Disagreement does not necessarily mean a group is failing; it can reveal issues that need to be discussed.

I recognise the importance of listening carefully instead of assuming that my own approach is always the best one. If disagreements arise, I would aim to discuss the issue calmly, focus on the task rather than the person and consider solutions that support the group's overall objective. If necessary, the group could revisit the assignment instructions to guide its decision.

♡

My lesson: Respectful disagreement can lead to better decisions when members are willing to listen.

03

BUILDING TRUST

Norming: Creating a Shared Approach

In the norming stage, members begin to develop clearer working relationships and agree on how to cooperate. Responsibilities become better understood, communication can improve and members may become more comfortable sharing ideas or asking for assistance.

I believe that agreeing on deadlines, deciding how progress will be communicated and respecting each person's contribution are important ways to build trust. A group can also check its progress regularly so that concerns are identified before they become bigger problems. Fair participation and mutual support help everyone remain connected to the shared goal.

♡

My lesson: Trust develops when people communicate clearly and consistently fulfil their responsibilities.

04

WORKING TOWARDS SUCCESS

Performing: Achieving Shared Goals

During the performing stage, a group is able to work more effectively towards its objectives. Members understand their roles, coordinate their efforts and focus on completing the task. They may also support one another when difficulties arise.

Effective performance requires more than completing individual sections. Members should review one another's contributions, check that the work follows the assignment requirements and make sure the final submission is consistent. Time management, accountability and constructive feedback are particularly valuable at this stage.

♡

My lesson: A successful group combines individual effort with shared responsibility for the final result.

05

CLOSING THE CHAPTER

Adjourning: Reflecting on the Experience

The adjourning stage occurs when a group completes its task and its members prepare to move on. It provides an opportunity to recognise what has been accomplished, evaluate how the team worked and identify areas for improvement in future projects.

After completing a group assignment, I would consider whether responsibilities were clear, communication was effective and deadlines were managed well. I would also reflect on the skills I developed and the approaches I would change next time. Recognising the contributions of others is important because it acknowledges that the outcome depended on shared effort.

♡

My lesson: Every completed task can provide insights that improve future collaboration.

MY PERSONAL ACTION PLAN

The teammate I
want to become.

I want to become a dependable team member who contributes ideas, respects other people's perspectives and takes responsibility for assigned work. To develop in this area, I will focus on the following actions:

  • ♡ Communicate early and clearly about tasks and deadlines.
  • ♡ Listen to different ideas before reaching decisions.
  • ♡ Complete my responsibilities and ask for help when needed.
  • ♡ Give and receive constructive feedback respectfully.
  • ♡ Reflect on group outcomes to improve future teamwork.

FINAL REFLECTION

Different strengths.
Shared growth.

Reflecting on Tuckman's model has helped me recognise that effective teamwork develops through communication, cooperation and learning. Although every group is different and may not move through the stages in a fixed order, the model provides a useful framework for understanding common team experiences.

As I continue my academic journey, I want to apply these lessons by being more intentional about communication, taking responsibility for my work and respecting the contributions of others. These abilities will support me not only in my studies but also in my future professional career.
